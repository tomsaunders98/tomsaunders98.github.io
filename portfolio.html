The Telegraph

Accountant for collapsed £2bn shadow bank had just two employees (Article)

I was the first journalist to reveal the connection between Market Financial Solutions and Magus Chartered Accountants, who have gone to be accused of playing a key role in the alleged fraud that took place at the bridging lender.

Shadow banking tycoon’s company wrongly claimed FCA approval (Article)

I discovered that before Market Financial Solutions solicited money from institutional clients, it operated a separate business that solicited money from individuals in the British Asian community. It claimed for years to have a licence from the FCA to operate the investment funds, but did not actually posses one.

Lloyds stops opening bank accounts for customers at branches (Article)

I revealed that Lloyds Bank was planning a major overhaul of the services it offers to individuals in branches, drastically cutting the number of services that could be performed in branch. The exclusive was my first front page story for The Telegraph.

Gambling tycoon moves property empire offshore ahead of Reeves tax raid (Article)

After compiling a list of family offices registered at Companies House, I uncovered that the owner of Betfred - Britain’s biggest taxpayer last year - had moved a property trading company owned by him to a trust in Jersey. The move came just weeks before changes to business property relief came into force, which would have made the business eligible for inheritance tax.

The Times

Award-winning

🏆 Residents count the £4bn cost of councils’ bad property bets (Article)

I investigated the property investments that had led four councils in close proximity on the commuter belt to amass around £4 billion in debt, or around £10,000 per resident. I submitted this piece as part of my MHP 30 under 30 award submission for business journalism, for which I won the highest award in the category.

🏆 Russia sanctions regime at risk of ‘falling to pieces’ (Article)

I led an investigation into Russian ownership of property in London and uncovered that the eight-year-old daughter of one of Vladimir Putin’s former regional governors served as the nominal owner of a £2.3 million flat in Kensington. I submitted this piece as part of my MHP 30 under 30 award submission for business journalism, for which I won the highest award in the category.

🏆 How a British firm sold tank tech to Russia after the invasion (Article)

I led an investigation into a Scottish microchip manufacturer that, after it was taken over by a company linked to the Chinese state, had supplied semiconductors to Russia later found in the country’s tanks. Following the article, the government used the National Security Act to force China to divest its stake - only the third time the act has been used in that manner in its history. I submitted this piece as part of my MHP 30 under 30 award submission for business journalism, for which I won the highest award in the category.

🏆 Russia’s ‘shadow fleet’ fuels lucrative trade in old tankers (Article)

I led The Times’ submission to a pan-Europe project which involved newsrooms across 13 countries identifying European shipping companies which had sold oil tankers that later ended up in Russia’s shadow fleet. We focused on Union Maritime, a British shipping company, that sold at least eight of its older vessels after Russia’s invasion of Ukraine which were then put to work by new owners in the shadow fleet. The project won the European Union’s Daphne Caruana Galizia Prize for Journalism.

Finance / Sanctions

UK suppliers send $100m of key equipment to Russia (Article)

I broke the story about the extent to which UK suppliers were exporting critical components, including semiconductors and drones, to Russia during 2023.

Raspberry Pi takes fight to firms selling its devices into Russia (Article)

I interviewed the CEO of Raspberry Pi about how the company has fought the flow of its products to Russia during the Ukraine war.

City watchdog fails to act on director bans (Article)

I uncovered the fact that seven disqualified directors still appeared as certified individuals under the FCA’s senior managers and certification regime. I wrote this article having only spent barely more than a month on the business desk at the time and it served as the section’s splash.

How one ‘Dudeist priest’ caused chaos with 800 rogue Companies House filings (Article)

After hundreds of companies house filings were successfully submitted to some of the UK’s largest financial institutions, speculation arose that they could be the product of either a highly sophisticated hack or a critical error within Companies House. After tracking down the origin of the scam, I was able to uncover and interview the individual behind the rogue filings.

Deepfake fraudsters impersonate FTSE chief executives (Article)

I helped to write an article about the rise in AI-driven impersonations of chief executives as part of highly sophisticated scams. The article included exclusive testimony from one FTSE 250 CEO about his experience being targeted by scammers.

What should government do with billions worth of seized bitcoin? (Article)

I wrote about how the government, through a series of seizures, had become the custodian of bitcoins worth over £3 billion. I explored how the UK might sell the bitcoins and how new laws governing their seizure and sale differed to those in Germany and the US.

The i

Stories

🏆Channel migrants (Project 1, Project 2) R [December 2021] - Nominated for DRUM award, Best Investigation

I used GIS ship tracking data from MarineTraffic during the course of the search for the migrant boat in order to identify key details about the search for the missing migrants. I used the R SF package to convert the raw GIS data into SHP files. From this, Dean Kirby and I were able to work out the methods used for searching for the migrants as well as the key players involved with the investigation.

How Le Pen won in the majority Muslim island of Mayotte (Project) R [April 2022]

While analysing the commune-level returns from the first round of the French presidential election I discovered that Marine Le Pen's biggest win was in the overseas department of Mayotte, an island which is 98 per cent Muslim. I then spoke to people in Mayotte to discover why Le Pen had become so popular in the island despite her Islamophobic history.

Russian misinformation (Project) Python, R, Gephi [March 2022]

I used the Twitter API along with Selenium to identify Russian embassy accounts that were spreading misinformation, despite the same tweets being deleted from other accounts. I then used Selenium to identify a large number of embassy accounts that were not labelled as government-affiliated organisations, in violation of Twitter’s own policy on labelling accounts.

The story behind the petrol shortage (Project) [October 2021]

Using data on fuel supply at forecourts from BEIS I explained how the fuel shortage was more akin to a conventional bank run, rather than an actual long term shortage. The article was retweeted almost a thousand times and was commented on and shared by multiple Conservative MPs.

Record number of NHS staff quit (Project) R [January 2021]

I broke the story that a record number of NHS staff had quit during the pandemic, despite many staff staying on during the first few months of the pandemic. I used R to analyse the data and spoke to multiple NHS staff members about the difficulties they faced during the pandemic and why some had chosen to quit.

Immensa Labs (Project) R [October 2021]

I used exclusive UKHSA data provided to me by an academic to detect which areas of England had been worst affected by the Immensa testing lab scandal. From there I used routine Covid-19 data to demonstrate how the most affected local authorities had seen a massive rise in Covid-19 cases in the wake of the scandal which was not replicated elsewhere in the country.
